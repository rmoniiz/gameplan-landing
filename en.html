FROM TRAINING TO MATCHDAY

ONE GAME PROBLEM. A WEEK OF COACHING DECISIONS.

Suppose you want to attack after regaining the ball. Define what players should look for, choose a task and plan when to train it. On matchday, record what you observe; use that evidence to decide what needs more work.

COACHING EXAMPLE · ADAPT TO YOUR TEAM

INTENTION
Attack after regaining possession.
BEHAVIOUR
The supporting player moves outside the defender’s field of vision.
TASK
Use the 2v1 to practise carrying or passing in response to the defender.
WEEK
Plan two sessions around that behaviour, with the match on Sunday.
OBSERVE & REVIEW

04 / PLAYERS

KNOW WHO TO LOOK AT MORE CLOSELY.

Keep each player's observations, tactical test responses and attendance history together. Review them alongside their training and match context before deciding what support they need. None of those records, on its own, proves development.

05 / MATCH

OBSERVE THE MATCH WITH A PURPOSE.

Use the match clock, pitch zones and action controls to record what happens. Return to the behaviour you trained: did the supporting player become available after possession was regained? Interpret the records in the context of the match.

06 / REFLECTION

BRING THE MATCH BACK TO THE NEXT DECISION.

Return to what you observed, consider the context and record the next work. You decide what to keep, adjust or investigate.

WHY GAMEPLAN EXISTS

THE WORK WAS SPREAD ACROSS TOO MANY PLACES.

My relationship with football began with my grandfather, Adão, an amateur coach. I started coaching in the United States in 2017 and later worked across different clubs and football environments before moving to the Netherlands.

In practice, the game model, weekly plan, training notes and match review lived in different places. Returning to a decision meant rebuilding the context around it.

GamePlan started as a way to keep that process connected. The platform organises the references; the methodology, reading of the game and technical decisions stay with the coach.
